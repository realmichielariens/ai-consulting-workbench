Acme Manufacturing Corp. is a mid-size industrial manufacturer (approximately 1,800
employees, $410M annual revenue) headquartered in Dayton, Ohio, with three plants
in the US Midwest and a smaller contract-manufacturing site in Monterrey, Mexico.
Acme makes precision-machined components for the agricultural and construction
equipment industries, selling mostly through long-term supply contracts with three
large OEM customers who together account for roughly 70% of revenue.

A new CIO, Priya Raman, joined eight weeks ago from a larger automotive-parts
supplier, replacing a long-tenured CIO who retired. She inherited an ERP landscape
built around a heavily customized, 14-year-old on-premise instance of a legacy
manufacturing ERP, integrated over the years with a patchwork of shop-floor MES
systems, a homegrown quoting tool and a separate finance/GL system that does not
share a common customer or part master with the ERP. Plant managers at each site
have, over time, built their own spreadsheet-based workarounds for scheduling and
inventory visibility because the ERP's own modules for those functions were judged
too slow or too rigid.

The CIO has been asked by the CEO and board to scope an ERP modernization
initiative, motivated by three pressures: the incumbent ERP vendor has announced
end-of-mainstream-support in three years, a major OEM customer has started asking
about real-time production-status visibility as a condition of contract renewal,
and the current system cannot support a planned expansion into a fourth product
line without significant custom development. The CIO wants a fact-based, structured
approach to scoping the initiative before committing to a vendor or a big-bang
replacement, and specifically wants to avoid another multi-year, over-customized
implementation like the current one.
