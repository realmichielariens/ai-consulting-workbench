# Acme Manufacturing Corp. — Engagement Context

Acme Manufacturing Corp. is a mid-size industrial manufacturer (approximately
1,800 employees, $410M annual revenue), headquartered in Dayton, Ohio, with
three plants in the US Midwest and a smaller contract-manufacturing site in
Monterrey, Mexico. Acme makes precision-machined components for the
agricultural and construction equipment industries, selling mostly through
long-term supply contracts with three large OEM customers who together
account for roughly 70% of revenue.

## Why this engagement

The CEO, Frank Delgado, has asked us to develop an IT strategy for Acme: a
board deck, due in **six weeks**, that the steering committee can endorse.
The scope is the whole of IT, not a single system decision — but four
pressures are what put IT strategy on the board's agenda this year, and they
will shape most of what Discovery and Synthesis surface:

1. **ERP end-of-support.** The incumbent vendor has announced
   end-of-mainstream-support for Acme's core ERP in three years. The
   instance is a heavily customized, 14-year-old on-premise deployment,
   integrated over the years with a patchwork of shop-floor MES systems, a
   homegrown quoting tool, and a separate finance/GL system that does not
   share a common customer or part master with the ERP.
2. **OEM real-time visibility.** One of the three large OEM customers has
   started asking about real-time production-status visibility as a
   condition of contract renewal. Acme's current systems were not built to
   report that way.
3. **Plant-level workarounds.** Plant managers at each site have, over
   time, built their own spreadsheet-based workarounds for scheduling and
   inventory visibility, because the ERP's own modules for those functions
   were judged too slow or too rigid. This keeps the plants running, but it
   is fragile and it hides risk from the center.
4. **A fourth product line.** The board wants to expand into a fourth
   product line. The current systems cannot support that expansion without
   significant custom development, on top of the customization Acme is
   already carrying.

## Sponsor and stance

Priya Raman, the CIO, has been in the role for **three months**. She was
hired from a larger automotive-parts supplier specifically to bring a more
disciplined approach to IT; she inherited the ERP landscape and its
customizations rather than having built them. She has asked for a
fact-based, structured strategy rather than a vendor-led sales process, and
she is explicit that she wants to avoid another multi-year, over-customized
implementation like the one she inherited.

## Deliverable and timeline

A board deck presenting the IT strategy — the major themes from Discovery,
the initiatives that follow from them, and a prioritized roadmap — endorsed
by the steering committee, in six weeks from kickoff. Discovery (interview
guide, then the CEO/CFO/COO/CIO interviews) needs to move quickly to leave
enough runway for synthesis, initiative-shaping and roadmap work before the
steerco date.
