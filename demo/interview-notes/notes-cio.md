# Interview notes — CIO

**Date:** Fri 9 Oct 2026
**Interviewer:** Jordan Casey (engagement lead)
**Attendees:** Priya Raman (CIO), Dev Anand (Director of IT, Applications —
joined for the second half), Jordan Casey
**Duration:** 60 minutes

## Key points

**Three months in.** Priya described her early read of the landscape: "the
ERP isn't the disease, it's a symptom — everyone kept solving local
problems (a plant's scheduling gap, a one-off report for an OEM) with a
customization, and fourteen years of that is what we have now."

**The history she's inherited.** The 14-year-old ERP was heavily
customized from day one by a team that has since left Acme; several
now-departed staff made undocumented changes over the years, and Dev Anand
is one of the few people left who understands parts of it. There was one
earlier attempt, roughly eight years ago, to replace core systems — the
same one independently referenced by the CEO and the COO — that ran about
eighteen months over budget and was partially rolled back. Priya has asked
Dev to compile what's known about why it failed before she commits to a
direction.

**The vision she's forming.** She wants to move the core toward something
less customized and more standard, and leans toward a cloud/SaaS direction
specifically for the ERP core — not because cloud is fashionable, but
because a standard cloud product is harder to quietly accumulate
customization debt on than an on-prem system a lean IT team fully
controls. "If it's cloud and standard, we can't quietly bolt on eleven
more one-off changes over the next decade the way we did last time."

**MES/GL integration.** She's frustrated that the ERP, the shop-floor MES
systems and the finance GL system don't share master data, and sees this
as a root cause behind both the plants' spreadsheet workarounds and the
reconciliation pain Grace (CFO) described independently.

**Urgency and sequencing.** She agrees the three-year end-of-support clock
is real and shouldn't be ignored, but — like the COO, though for a
different reason — wants a phased, sequenced roadmap rather than a single
big-bang replacement. Her reason is avoiding a repeat of the failed
attempt eight years ago, not primarily the plants' seasonal risk, though
she said the two concerns "point the same direction anyway."

**The OEM ask.** She sees the real-time visibility request as a legitimate
forcing function that could help justify the investment case to the board,
if it's framed as part of the broader strategy rather than a bolt-on
project.

**Dev Anand (joining partway).** Confirmed the institutional-knowledge
risk directly: "there are customizations in that ERP that only make sense
if you know which plant manager asked for them in 2014, and some of those
people are gone."
