# Interview notes — CFO

**Date:** Wed 7 Oct 2026
**Interviewer:** Jordan Casey (engagement lead)
**Attendees:** Grace Whitfield (CFO), Jordan Casey
**Duration:** ~35 minutes (cut short — Grace was pulled into an urgent
board-audit call partway through)

## Key points

**GL/ERP mismatch.** Finance runs a separate GL system that does not share
a customer or part master with the ERP. Reconciliation between the two is
manual and happens every month-end; Grace estimates it costs her team
roughly a week of effort each close.

**Past pain.** Grace independently referenced the same prior systems
initiative Frank mentioned (roughly eight years ago). She said finance
absorbed a disproportionate share of the overrun because the
customizations kept touching financial reporting. She was explicit about
her wariness of "another multi-year build where nobody can tell me what
we're actually buying."

**Fourth product line.** In her view this is the most financially
consequential initiative on the board's agenda this year. She said it's
hard to build a clean financial case for it today because costing data for
a new line would have to be bolted onto the existing customizations —
"which is exactly the kind of thing that always costs more than the
estimate."

**Cloud — only briefly touched.** She mentioned, in passing, that "anything
that gets us out of buying more servers is a conversation I'll have," but
we did not get far enough to understand what she'd actually want from a
cloud or on-prem approach, or how strongly she holds that view.

**Audit and controls.** Before the call was cut short, she flagged that
whatever replaces or touches the GL integration needs a clean audit trail
from day one — she said the current manual reconciliation at least leaves
a paper trail, even if it's slow, and she doesn't want a faster system that
makes it harder for the auditors to follow the numbers.

**Interviewer note — gap.** The call ended before we reached budget
appetite: we did not get to how Grace would want a program like this
funded or staged across fiscal years, what capital versus operating
spending mix she'd prefer, or what number would make her uncomfortable.
**Flagged for a follow-up conversation** — this is a real gap, not
something to infer from the rest of the interview.

## Quotes

> "That's exactly the kind of thing that always costs more than the
