# Interview notes — COO

**Date:** Thu 8 Oct 2026
**Interviewer:** Jordan Casey (engagement lead)
**Attendees:** Marta Ilić (COO), Jordan Casey
**Duration:** 60 minutes

## Key points

**Harvest-season peak.** Production across all three US plants ramps hard
during a defined seasonal window each year. Marta was adamant that no core
system change should touch the plants during that window: "if the ERP so
much as hiccups during peak, we miss OEM ship dates, and that's worse than
anything a slow strategy costs us."

**Sequencing.** If ERP replacement happens, she wants it to start after
the seasonal peak and to be phased plant-by-plant rather than a single
cutover across all three sites at once. She referenced the same prior
systems initiative the CEO and CFO mentioned: "big bang across three
plants at once is how the last one went sideways."

**Spreadsheet workarounds.** She pushed back on treating the plants'
spreadsheet workarounds (the most developed one is Dayton's) as shadow IT
to be shut down. In her words, the Dayton spreadsheet is "the thing
actually keeping scheduling honest day to day," and she wants it treated
as evidence of a functional requirement the real system currently fails to
meet, not just decommissioned.

**Cloud — skeptical, specifically for the shop floor.** Plant network
connectivity has had real reliability problems: she referenced a
multi-hour WAN outage last year that stalled MES reporting at two sites.
She does not want core production scheduling or MES dependent on external
connectivity "unless someone can guarantee our network first," and
explicitly distinguished this from back-office systems, which she's more
open to seeing move to the cloud.

**OEM real-time visibility.** She doesn't disagree with the goal, but is
worried the request becomes an unfunded mandate on the plants — more
reporting expected from her teams without new tooling to produce it.

**Owen Butler.** She brought him up unprompted as someone who should be
consulted directly before any scheduling or inventory tooling changes are
finalized — not just interviewed for context, but involved in reviewing
whatever the strategy proposes, since he'd spot gaps between what a new
system claims to do and what the plant floor actually needs day to day.

## Quotes

> "Ask me again after peak season and I'll tell you cloud is fine for
> finance. Ask me about the shop floor and my answer's different."

> "Whatever you put in that deck, the plants cannot be the ones absorbing
> the risk of getting it done fast."

> "Big bang across three plants at once is how the last one went
> sideways."
